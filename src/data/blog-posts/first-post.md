This is *not* my first blog post. I have written several before, but nothing I have ever published, and I have never gotten very far. Maybe a paragraph or two before I felt the overwhelming sense that what I was writing was too cheesy, or not novel enough, or would not show me to the world in the way that I wanted to be seen. Every time I sat down to write a blog post, there has been that critic in the back of my mind.
![Sketch of someone writing with critical sentences around them; "Well that was obvious", "Is this even interesting?" and "This is pretentious"](/assets/blog/personal_critic.png)

It's cliché, but we are often our own worst critic. So why should I want to write a blog? The question is two parts really. Why write, and why publish your writing?

## Why write?
To start with, regular writing is incredibly beneficial. It is the process of organising your thoughts, getting them down on paper, and activating your brain. It solidifies concepts in your mind and helps you work through your problems. These are the advantages I have had in mind whenever I have approached writing a blog in the past. So why hasn't it happened? Because of that inner critic. Because distractions are everywhere, and we love to put off doing the hard thing. I'm sure I'll get around to it someday. 

## Why publish what I write?
There are a number of reasons. I am a software developer with over a decade of experience working on the web, so I should probably have a presence on the web. Not to be haughty, but I think some of the things I think and do are interesting enough to share. But the best reason in my mind is because it scares me. I am often paralysed into inaction when simply replying to a group chat of my closest friends or family, so putting something up where anyone with an internet connection can see it is quite frankly terrifying. But conquering that fear is precisely the reason why I think this is worthwhile doing. Talking about oneself is hard, but there's really only one way to get better.

The impetus for writing at this very moment is the fastai fastbook course and Rachel Thomas' blog post, [Why you (yes, you) should blog](https://medium.com/@racheltho/why-you-yes-you-should-blog-7d2544ac1045). I also think now more than ever is an important time to be writing. As the task of thinking is so easily handed off to LLMs as they get more and more capable, I think it is important to maintain some creativity and exercise my brain. Therefore it is important to me that none of this is drafted, written, or reviewed by AI. Will the quality suffer? Probably! But in the era of LLMs, it feels like a small act of rebellion to go without them. With that said, the inspiration for why I'm writing can't be forgotten, so I may be writing about AI from time to time. Which leads on to the next question.

## What will I write about?
Great question! I don't know yet. But stick with me and we will find out. A few of my interests will influence where my writing leads me. Rather than give a list of what those are and promise something that might never happen, I'll let the blog posts themselves illuminate my interests when that happens. For now, I am writing purely for myself, as a creative outlet and a tool for self-examination and growth. I hope you will join for the journey.

Or not, who cares 🤷‍♂️
